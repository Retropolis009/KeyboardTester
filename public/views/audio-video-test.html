Microphone Oscilloscope & Peak Meter

Peak level — −∞ dBFS

Speak at normal volume. A healthy headset peaks between −18 dBFS and −6 dBFS. A meter pinned at −60 dBFS means the OS has muted or the wrong input is selected; clipping near 0 dBFS means the input gain is too high.

Stereo Speaker Test

Phase inversion and wiring faults show up as a hollow, distant centre image. If the centred test sounds thin but one channel alone sounds full, the speakers are wired out of phase or the 3.5 mm plug is half-seated.

Webcam Frame Rate & Resolution

Resolution is read from the actual video track settings, and FPS is counted from requestVideoFrameCallback where supported. Motion blur or a stuck frame indicates a driver or USB bandwidth problem.

Running a Private Mic and Webcam Test Online

A mic and webcam test online only works well when it is honest about where your data goes. KeyPulse requests the microphone and camera through getUserMedia, routes the stream into a Web Audio analyser and a local <video> element, and never uploads a byte. Closing the tab or pressing Stop releases the device immediately, and the browser's own indicator light is the ground truth that capture has ended.

Start with the oscilloscope: a flat line at silence that reacts to speech confirms the device is live. If the meter never moves, the browser may be using a different default input than the one you expect — pick the device explicitly from the input selector. Headset microphones often expose separate "communications" and "music" endpoints.

Interpreting the dBFS Peak Meter

dBFS is decibels relative to full scale, so 0 dBFS is digital clipping. Speech that peaks around −18 to −6 dBFS transcribes well in calls and leaves headroom for loud moments. A signal that clips carries harsh distortion no noise gate can repair, while a signal hovering near −55 dBFS will sound muffled to everyone else. The health readout summarises the last second of samples into Silent, Healthy, Hot, or Clipping.

Stereo Speaker Sweeps and Camera Frame Rates

The tone generator uses an oscillator with a stereo panner, so a left-only tone proves the left speaker and left channel of the cable independently. A camera that reports 1920×1080 but only 15 FPS is usually limited by USB 2.0 bandwidth or by another application holding an exclusive lock.
