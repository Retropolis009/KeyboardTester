How to Diagnose Defective Mouse Microswitches & Scroll Encoders

A dedicated mouse tester online quickly separates a software double-click-speed setting from physical wear inside Omron, Kailh, Huano, or TTC microswitches. When the internal copper leaf loses tension, one finger click produces two electrical pulses fewer than 50 milliseconds apart, and the OS registers a phantom second press.

Click deliberately and watch the double-click fault counter. A clean switch produces one actuation per press regardless of how fast you click; a failing switch trips the fault counter even at slow cadence, which is the definitive symptom that a replacement switch or RMA is justified.

Measuring Real USB Mouse Polling Rate in Hz

Gaming mice advertise 1000 Hz, 2000 Hz, or 4000 Hz report intervals, while office mice typically report at 125 Hz. The polling readout samples pointer movement events, keeps the fastest plausible inter-report delta, and snaps the estimate to the standard USB HID buckets. A browser cannot read the USB descriptor directly, so the figure is an observed estimate — but it reliably distinguishes a 125 Hz membrane-era mouse from a 1000 Hz gaming sensor. For APEX-style click benchmarks, use the same pad and watch peak CPS climb.

Checking Optical vs. Mechanical Scroll Encoders

Roll the wheel steadily upward inside the pad. If downward steps increment during an upward roll, dust inside the rotary encoder is interrupting the quadrature phase signals.
