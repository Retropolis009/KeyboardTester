Why Use an Online Keyboard Tester for Mechanical & Laptop Keyboards?

Whether you just finished a hot-swap build, spilled liquid near a laptop palm rest, or keep losing inputs mid-match, an online keyboard tester verifies every switch matrix position in seconds without installing untrusted executables. KeyPulse reads raw hardware scan codes from the browser's KeyboardEvent.code rather than the typed character, so left and right modifiers stay distinct and Numpad digits never masquerade as the number row.

The bench tracks per-key actuation counts, peak simultaneous rollover, hold duration, and the time between release and the next press of the same key. Everything is rendered into the deck above and can be exported as a JSON or plain-text diagnostic report you can attach to a warranty ticket or a r/buildapc troubleshooting post.

Testing N-Key Rollover (NKRO), Ghosting & Matrix Blocking

Budget membrane keyboards share rows without per-key diodes, so simultaneous presses collide into 2KRO or 6KRO behaviour. Hold several keys at once and watch the Rollover readout to confirm unlimited NKRO actuation. Pressing keys in a diagonal pattern quickly exposes ghosting on cheap controllers. For the theory behind the matrix, read NKRO vs. 6KRO keyboard ghosting.

Automatic Switch Chatter & Double-Typing Detection

Worn copper leaves and over-aggressive 1 ms debounce profiles cause a single press to fire twice within 5–30 ms. Every keycap carries a bounce monitor and actuation counter here; for per-event interval histograms and custom debounce thresholds open the dedicated switch chatter lab. Keys held longer than four seconds trip the stuck-key watchdog so a failing switch or a jammed keycap is flagged before you finish a sentence.

Scan Rate, Lock LEDs & Key Location Readouts

The scan-rate estimate samples timestamp deltas between consecutive discrete hardware events and snaps the fastest plausible interval to the standard USB HID buckets of 125 Hz, 250 Hz, 500 Hz, and 1000 Hz. It is an observed estimate from the browser event stream, not a USB descriptor read, but it reliably separates an aging membrane board from a gaming keyboard polling a full millisecond. The Caps, Num, and Scroll indicators mirror the OS lock state reported by the keyboard itself, and the location readout separates left-modifier, right-modifier, and Numpad variants that share a printed legend.
